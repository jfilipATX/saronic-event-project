# audience_estimate_reasoning

**Prompt**

For a company-hosted Saronic product launch in Austin, Texas targeting defense primes, DoD program offices, and trade press: propose a conservative, a baseline, and an ambitious attendance estimate. For each, give the number and one sentence justifying it. Note explicitly what would have to be true for the ambitious number to hold.

**Response**

## Three attendance scenarios

Assumes an invite-only, single-day launch at or near Saronic's Austin footprint, with a vessel/hardware reveal as the draw.

| Scenario | Total attendees | One-sentence justification |
|---|---|---|
| **Conservative** | **125–150** | Assumes government travel is constrained (CR/shutdown, year-end funds, no-cost-to-government travel not pre-arranged), so the room is mostly Austin-local: Saronic staff, Texas-based primes and suppliers, investors, state officials, and 6–10 press. |
| **Baseline** | **250–275** | Assumes a normal 8–10 week invite runway with a real hardware reveal, ~35% no-show on government RSVPs and ~15% on industry, drawing a mix of PEO/NAVSEA/DIU/SOCOM staff, prime BD and engineering leads, 12–18 trade press, and the local ecosystem. |
| **Ambitious** | **450–550** | Assumes the launch becomes the anchor event of a broader Austin defense-tech week and pulls DC-based flag/SES-level attendance plus national press, not just BD staff. |

## What must be true for the ambitious number to hold

1. **Calendar anchoring.** The date sits adjacent to a magnet event (e.g., an AUSA/SAS/AFA-adjacent week, a DIU or Navy industry day, or SXSW-timed defense programming) so DC travelers amortize one trip across several meetings. Standalone Austin events rarely justify a cross-country flight for a GS-14.
2. **A genuinely new capability, not a variant.** Press and program offices come for a first-look demo — ideally on-water — not a static display or a rendering.
3. **At least one senior government or congressional confirmation announced early.** A named flag officer, service acquisition executive, or Texas delegation member on the agenda is the single biggest driver of secondary RSVPs; without it the ambitious tier collapses toward baseline.
4. **Ethics path cleared in advance.** A written "widely attended gathering" determination (or a no-meal/attendee-pays structure) is in hand early enough for government invitees to get ethics counsel sign-off. This alone kills DoD attendance more often than interest does.
5. **No fiscal disruption.** No shutdown, CR-driven travel freeze, or Q4 travel-budget exhaustion in the 60 days before the date.
6. **Venue and logistics can absorb it.** Parking, badging/access control for a facility that may have controlled areas, on-water viewing capacity, and a plan for classified vs. unclassified conversation boundaries at 500 people.
7. **Real press incentive.** Embargoed briefing materials and 1:1 executive availability, or trade press covers it remotely from the release instead of flying in.

## Trade-offs in choosing a planning number

- **Build to conservative:** lowest sunk cost, but an overflow situation at a launch reads as poor planning to the exact primes you're courting, and you lose the "packed room" optics in press photos.
- **Build to baseline:** most defensible; use tiered catering and a venue with a releasable overflow block so you can flex up ~20% at two weeks out.
- **Build to ambitious:** protects the upside moment but risks a visibly half-empty space if any two of the seven conditions above fail — and the government-travel and fiscal conditions are the two you control least.

## Two things worth deciding early, since they move all three numbers

- **Whether Saronic employees count toward the total.** A few hundred local staff can make the room look full or can crowd out the guests you actually want talking to each other. Worth setting an explicit internal-attendance cap.
- **Single-tier vs. two-tier event.** A smaller invite-only executive/program-office session plus a larger open reception lets you optimize the conservative and ambitious goals simultaneously rather than betting on one number.

I'd suggest firming the date-anchoring question (#1) and the ethics determination (#4) before locking a venue size — those two answers will tell you which of these three you're actually planning.
